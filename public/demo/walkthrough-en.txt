This is Slot, a booking application for a sports coach. I’m opening an isolated client demo. The people are fictional, but reservations use the real booking service and database.

I choose an available session. The summary shows the date, time, duration and price, with the calendar’s timezone visible. The client can check the details before reserving.

The reservation starts as a ten-minute hold. I confirm the clearly labelled demo payment, without charging a card. The booking is now confirmed. Abandoned holds expire and release their place.

In My sessions, I move the booking to another available time. After confirmation, the new time appears. The server checks availability again when rescheduling.

Cancelling asks for confirmation, then displays the cancelled status. The place is released. These states control availability; they are not just decorative labels.

Switching to the coach reveals the timetable and booking management. This role switch belongs to the demo. The server still checks permissions for protected operations.

Finally, two clients request the last place simultaneously. One succeeds and the other is safely refused. This exercises the real booking service. This recording uses the local application and simulated checkout.
